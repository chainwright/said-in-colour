If you have red-green colour blindness, buying a shirt online usually means asking someone else.

The photo doesn't settle it, and the label makes it worse. "Dusty rose", "sage", "stone" mean nothing when those colours collapse into each other. About 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women live with this, and plenty of them give up and wear black, navy and grey every day.

So we built Said in Colour. You try a shirt on with YouCam's AI Clothes, and the app shows you the same picture twice: inside a lens, what you see; outside it, what everyone else sees. It names the colour both ways. Every shirt in the case gets a tag (trap, careful or safe) worked out from its measured colour.

The colour-vision model comes from published research (Machado et al., 2009) and runs on every pixel. It's a shopping aid, not a medical test.

We made it for the YouCam API hackathon on Devpost. The demo video and the live app are in the first comment.
